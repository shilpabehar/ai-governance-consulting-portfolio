# AI Governance Consulting Methodology

This section defines the consulting methodology used across the AI governance, responsible AI, AI risk, and AI assurance case studies in this portfolio.

The approach is designed to assess how organizations govern AI systems across their lifecycle, identify AI-related risks, evaluate existing controls, and recommend practical governance improvements.

## Consulting Lifecycle

**Discover → Assess → Identify Risks → Design Controls → Assure → Implement → Monitor → Improve**

### 1. Discover

Understand the organization's AI landscape, business objectives, AI use cases, stakeholders, technology, users, intended outcomes, and operating environment.

### 2. Assess

Assess the current governance framework, policies, processes, roles, responsibilities, controls, and available evidence.

### 3. Identify Risks

Identify risks associated with the AI system, including technical, operational, legal, privacy, cybersecurity, safety, ethical, regulatory, and business risks.

### 4. Design Controls

Define practical controls to reduce identified risks, including human oversight, data controls, AI evaluation, monitoring, documentation, approval gates, accountability mechanisms, and lifecycle controls.

### 5. Assure

Evaluate whether the proposed or existing controls are:

* Appropriate for the identified risks
* Implemented as intended
* Supported by objective evidence
* Effective in achieving their intended outcomes

### 6. Implement

Support the organization in operationalizing recommended governance processes, controls, roles, and decision mechanisms.

### 7. Monitor

Establish ongoing monitoring for AI performance, risks, incidents, changes, control effectiveness, and emerging issues.

### 8. Improve

Use monitoring results, incidents, assessments, stakeholder feedback, and lessons learned to continuously improve the AI governance framework.

## Frameworks Used

The consulting assessments in this portfolio use three complementary frameworks:

### ISO/IEC 42001

Used primarily to assess the organization's **AI Management System (AIMS)**, including governance, leadership, planning, operational controls, performance evaluation, and continual improvement.

### NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF)

Used to assess practical AI risk management across the lifecycle through:

**GOVERN → MAP → MEASURE → MANAGE**

### ISO/IEC 23894

Used as supporting guidance for detailed AI risk management, including:

**Risk Identification → Risk Analysis → Risk Evaluation → Risk Treatment → Residual Risk → Monitoring & Review**

These frameworks are used together rather than treated as independent checklists.

## Consulting Assessment Principle

The assessment distinguishes between three levels of governance maturity:

**Documented → Implemented → Effective**

A governance requirement documented in a policy does not automatically demonstrate that the corresponding process is being implemented.

Similarly, evidence that a control exists does not necessarily demonstrate that the control is effective.

The consulting assessment therefore considers:

1. **Is the requirement or control defined?**
2. **Is it implemented in practice?**
3. **Is there objective evidence?**
4. **Is the control effective?**

## Risk-Based Approach

Governance controls are proportionate to the nature and level of AI risk.

The assessment considers factors such as:

* Potential impact
* Severity of consequences
* Likelihood of occurrence
* Intended use
* User population
* Degree of automation
* Human oversight
* Regulatory and organizational requirements
* Safety or business criticality
* Data sensitivity
* Third-party dependencies
* Ability to detect and correct failures

Higher-risk AI use cases require stronger assurance, oversight, evidence, and approval mechanisms.

---

## Assurance Mindset

The portfolio applies an assurance-oriented mindset developed from safety-critical engineering and verification & validation practices.

Key principles include:

* Requirements and intended use should be clearly defined.
* Risks should be identified before controls are selected.
* Controls should be traceable to identified risks.
* AI outputs should be evaluated against defined expectations.
* Human oversight should be appropriate to the level of risk.
* Evidence should support governance decisions.
* Control effectiveness should be demonstrated rather than assumed.
* Changes to the AI system, data, model, or operating environment should trigger appropriate reassessment.

This portfolio contains a combination of:

* **Simulated consulting engagements** developed to demonstrate AI governance, responsible AI, AI risk management, and AI assurance capabilities.
* **Anonymized professional experience** based on real aerospace software V&V and engineering process-improvement activities, with organizational, customer, product, and proprietary details removed or generalized.

The portfolio does not disclose confidential, proprietary, customer-specific, or restricted organizational information.

Simulated engagements are clearly identified as portfolio exercises, while anonymized professional experience is presented at a level appropriate for demonstrating transferable consulting, governance, risk, and assurance capabilities.
