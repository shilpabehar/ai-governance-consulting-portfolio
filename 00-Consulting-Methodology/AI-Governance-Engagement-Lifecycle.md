# AI Governance Engagement Lifecycle

## Purpose

This lifecycle provides a structured approach for conducting AI governance, responsible AI, AI risk, and AI assurance consulting engagements.

It is designed to move from understanding the client's business and AI environment to assessing risks, designing controls, establishing governance, and providing actionable recommendations.

---

# Engagement Lifecycle

```text
DISCOVER
   ↓
ASSESS
   ↓
IDENTIFY RISKS
   ↓
ANALYZE & EVALUATE
   ↓
DESIGN CONTROLS
   ↓
ASSURE
   ↓
IMPLEMENT
   ↓
MONITOR
   ↓
IMPROVE
```

---

## 1. Discover

### Objective

Understand the client's business context, AI landscape, objectives, stakeholders, and intended use of AI.

### Key Activities

* Understand the business problem.
* Understand the AI use case.
* Identify intended users and affected parties.
* Understand the AI technology and architecture at an appropriate level.
* Identify internal and external stakeholders.
* Understand the AI lifecycle.
* Identify applicable organizational policies and external requirements.
* Establish engagement scope and objectives.

### Typical Outputs

* Engagement Scope
* AI System Profile
* Stakeholder Map
* AI Lifecycle Map
* Initial Evidence Request List
* Initial Assumptions and Open Questions

---

# 2. Assess

### Objective

Understand the client's current governance environment and determine what controls and processes already exist.

### Key Activities

* Review policies and procedures.
* Review existing AI governance documentation.
* Review risk assessments.
* Review roles and responsibilities.
* Review existing controls.
* Review AI development and deployment processes.
* Review available evidence.
* Map existing practices to applicable frameworks.

### Key Question

> What does the organization say it does, and what evidence demonstrates that it actually does it?

### Typical Outputs

* Governance Assessment
* ISO/IEC 42001 Mapping
* NIST AI RMF Mapping
* Evidence Matrix
* Preliminary Gap Assessment

---

# 3. Identify Risks

### Objective

Identify risks associated with the AI system and its operating environment.

### Risk Categories

Depending on the engagement, risks may include:

* Safety
* Security
* Privacy
* Reliability
* Accuracy
* Bias and fairness
* Transparency
* Explainability
* Hallucination
* Data quality
* Data leakage
* Human over-reliance
* Automation bias
* Regulatory compliance
* Third-party dependency
* Model drift
* Operational failure
* Business impact
* Reputational impact

### Typical Output

**AI Risk Register**

---

# 4. Analyze & Evaluate

### Objective

Determine the significance of identified risks and whether existing controls reduce the risks to an acceptable level.

### Key Activities

For each identified risk:

1. Determine potential consequences.
2. Assess likelihood using defined criteria and available evidence.
3. Determine inherent risk.
4. Identify existing controls.
5. Assess control effectiveness.
6. Determine residual risk.
7. Compare residual risk against defined acceptance criteria.
8. Determine whether additional treatment is required.

### Important Principle

Risk evaluation should not be based solely on intuition or a generic risk score.

Where possible, risk decisions should be supported by:

* Evaluation results
* Testing
* Historical evidence
* Incident data
* Expert assessment
* Business impact analysis
* Defined organizational risk criteria

### Typical Outputs

* Risk Analysis
* Risk Evaluation
* Residual Risk Assessment
* Risk Treatment Recommendations

---

# 5. Design Controls

### Objective

Design practical controls that address identified risks.

Controls may include:

* Human oversight
* Approval gates
* AI evaluation
* Data validation
* Version and baseline controls
* Access controls
* Monitoring
* Incident management
* Documentation
* Traceability
* Model or system testing
* Change management
* Supplier controls
* Periodic reassessment

### Control Design Principle

Every important control should have a clear relationship between:

**Risk → Control → Owner → Evidence → Effectiveness**

### Typical Output

**Risk-Control Matrix**

---

# 6. Assure

### Objective

Determine whether governance controls are appropriate, implemented, and effective.

### Key Activities

* Define assurance criteria.
* Define evaluation methods.
* Review objective evidence.
* Evaluate AI system performance where applicable.
* Assess human oversight.
* Test selected controls.
* Identify control weaknesses.
* Assess whether evidence supports the governance decision.

### Key Principle

**A documented control is not automatically an effective control.**

### Typical Outputs

* AI Evaluation Plan
* Evidence Matrix
* Control Effectiveness Assessment
* Assurance Findings
* Deployment / Approval Recommendation

---

# 7. Implement

### Objective

Translate recommendations into an operational governance model.

### Key Activities

* Define roles and responsibilities.
* Establish RACI.
* Define approval and escalation paths.
* Integrate governance into AI lifecycle processes.
* Establish documentation requirements.
* Establish risk review processes.
* Establish AI inventory and reporting mechanisms.
* Define operational procedures.

### Typical Outputs

* Governance Operating Model
* Governance RACI
* AI Lifecycle Governance Process
* Approval Workflow
* Escalation Process

---

# 8. Monitor

### Objective

Ensure that AI governance remains effective after implementation.

### Key Activities

Monitor:

* AI performance
* Risk indicators
* Incidents
* Complaints
* Control effectiveness
* Model or system changes
* Data changes
* Supplier changes
* Regulatory changes
* Emerging risks

### Typical Outputs

* AI Monitoring Plan
* AI Risk Dashboard
* Incident Management Process
* Periodic Review Process

---

# 9. Improve

### Objective

Continuously improve AI governance based on operational experience and new information.

### Inputs for Improvement

* Monitoring results
* AI incidents
* Audit findings
* Control failures
* Risk reassessments
* Stakeholder feedback
* New regulations
* Technology changes
* Lessons learned

### Typical Outputs

* Corrective Actions
* Preventive Actions
* Updated Controls
* Updated Risk Assessments
* Governance Improvements

# Consulting Evidence Model

Throughout the engagement, evidence should be evaluated at three levels:

```text
DOCUMENTED
    ↓
IMPLEMENTED
    ↓
EFFECTIVE
```

### Documented

The organization has defined the requirement, process, policy, or control.

### Implemented

The organization is actually performing the defined activity.

### Effective

There is evidence that the activity or control achieves its intended outcome.

This distinction is particularly important when assessing AI governance maturity.

---

# Consulting Deliverable Chain

The engagement should maintain traceability from the client's problem through to the final recommendation.

```text
Business Objective
       ↓
AI Use Case
       ↓
AI Risk
       ↓
Risk Analysis
       ↓
Risk Treatment
       ↓
Control
       ↓
Control Owner
       ↓
Evidence
       ↓
Effectiveness
       ↓
Recommendation
```

This provides a defensible basis for governance decisions and helps prevent recommendations from becoming disconnected from the actual business or AI risks.

---

# Final Engagement Output

A typical engagement should conclude with an executive-level assessment covering:

### Current State

What governance capabilities currently exist?

### Key Risks

What are the most significant AI-related risks?

### Control Gaps

Which controls are missing, insufficient, or ineffective?

### Priority Actions

What should be addressed immediately, in the near term, and later?

### Governance Model

Who owns AI governance, risk, assurance, and operational decisions?

### Assurance Position

Is the AI system ready for the intended stage of deployment, or are additional actions required?

### Monitoring

How will the organization know whether the governance controls remain effective?

---

# Consulting Principle

The objective of an AI governance engagement is not simply to determine whether an organization complies with a framework.

The objective is to help the organization establish a **practical, risk-based, evidence-driven governance system that enables responsible use of AI while maintaining appropriate accountability and oversight.**
